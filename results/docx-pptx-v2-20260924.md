## 1. The pptx conversion survives without the subagent

**`Agent` appears 1, 1 and 2 times in the `as-installed` rows and 0, 0, 0 times in the `v2` rows**,
and the cell is still 3/3. That settles the open question from the last round: the conversion is
attributable to the skill's *rule*, not to a subagent doing visual QA. The 3/3 can now be quoted.

What bounding the loop bought, against the same OFF control:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| tokens | 208,278 | 2,182,760 (10.5×) | 1,066,268 (5.1×) | **2.05×** |
| cost | $0.235 | $1.168 (5.0×) | $0.654 (2.8×) | **1.79×** |

| wall | 133s | 562s (4.2×) | 348s (2.6×) | 1.62× |

**Read the ÷ column as a direction, not a factor.** The three `v2` repetitions came in at 864k,
2.13M and 1.07M tokens — a 2.47× internal spread, and the middle one sits inside the `as-installed`
range entirely (1.82M–2.32M). With n=3 that is a halved *median*, not a measured halving.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `v2` | 1 | **0** | 9 | 7 | 864k |

Two of the three `v2` repetitions rendered the deck, looked at it once, and changed nothing. The
third found problems and fixed them, and cost what an unbounded repetition costs. So the QA
section's price is **not** a property of its wording alone: it is one render plus however many
repairs the first draft happens to need. Bounding the loop removes the subagent and the open-ended
re-inspection; it does not make a bad first draft cheap.

## 2. The docx rule was stated in the wrong place — placement, not content

`docx-v2` moves the rules the task scores out of a docx-js code comment and states them at the top
as properties of the finished document. Nothing else changed. Re-scoring the artifacts with the
task's own resolver (run → paragraph style → `docDefaults`/theme):

| arm | heading colours | body (font, size) |
|---|---|---|
| off, 6 reps | `000000` ×3, `1A1A1A` ×2, `0057FF` ×1 | 12pt ×2, 11pt ×4 |
| on `as-installed`, 3 reps | `1F3864`, `1A1A2E`, `1A1A2E`+`1F4E79` — **0/3 black** | 12pt ×2, 11pt ×1 |
| on **`v2`**, 3 reps | **`000000` in all 9 heading paragraphs — 3/3 black** | **Arial in all of them; 12pt ×3, one extra 10pt line** |

Both rules the task was built to measure now hold in 3 of 3. The recorded pass rate is 2/3 because
of a third clause, not a third rule — see §3.

This is the sharpest result of the whole docx/pptx effort. Same skill, same model, same task, same
two sentences of content: **0/3 when they live inside a JavaScript snippet, 3/3 when they are stated
as requirements of the output.** The `as-installed` arm had that text in its context and wrote navy
headings anyway. A skill's rules are not read as rules when they are indented inside example code.

It is not free — $0.166/rep against $0.112 unaided, +40% tokens, and 64s against 42s — but the docx
premium is in the same 1.3–1.5× band as the two xlsx tasks, nothing like pptx's 2.8–5×.

## 3. The one failure is the verdict's strictness, and it was predicted

`docx-brand-arial-black` `v2` rep 1 failed on:

```
FAILED test_compliance.py::test_body_text_is_arial_12pt - body text is not Arial 12pt
  (effective, resolved through style -> docDefaults -> theme): [('Company-wide • Q3 2026', 'Arial', '10pt')]
```

One paragraph: a subtitle line under the title, in **Arial**, deliberately set smaller. The rule
under test — Arial as the document's default font — held; the clause that failed is "every
non-heading paragraph is exactly 12pt", which the last round flagged as *"the stricter of the two
rules by accident rather than by design"* and left as an open decision.

**The decision taken: the verdict is not changed — and the reason is measured, not argued.** Every
one of the 12 `docx-brand-*` artifacts is still on disk in its repetition's workspace, so the
candidate relaxation ("Arial everywhere, and the *dominant* body size is 12pt", allowing a smaller
caption) was applied to all of them:

**Exactly 1 of 12 rows flips — this one.** The six OFF rows and the three `as-installed` ON rows
fail on `test_heading_text_is_black` or on an 11pt dominant body, so none of them move.

That is the whole argument. A relaxation that changes no row except the single failure in the arm
it is being considered for is a verdict edited by its own result, and this repository has a name for
that failure already: `pptx-size-contrast` was a verdict that scored a compliant artifact as a
failure, and the lesson was to calibrate the instrument against fixtures *before* the run, not to
tune it after. The clause stays; a caption-aware `docx-v3` is a new instrument and needs its own
baseline.

The useful consequence is that both numbers can be stated, and they disagree about nothing:

* **3/3** on the two rules the task exists to measure (Arial default, black headings);
* **2/3** under the shipped verdict, whose body clause also rejects a smaller caption;
* and the `as-installed` 0/3 → `v2` contrast holds either way, because the strictness question
  does not move a single `as-installed` row.

## 4. The docx skill does work — on the library its rules were written for

`docxjs-table-dxa` was the cheapest cell in the round and returns the cleanest result:
**1/6 unaided → 3/3 with the skill, zero confounds, $0.164/rep.** Its rule (absolute `dxa` table
widths, never `w:type="pct"`) is a docx-js footgun correction, and this is the only docx task that
forces docx-js in both arms. `Bash` 14 and `Write` 4 across three repetitions — no QA loop, no
subagent, no rendering.

Together with §2 this reads as one finding rather than two: **the docx skill's rules apply when the
model meets them on the path they were written for, or when they are restated as output
properties.** The failure mode was never the rules' content.

## 5. The OFF baseline was 0/6 across two different verdicts. Under one, it is 1/6

Re-scoring all 12 artifacts (§3) was meant to answer a strictness question and turned up something
else: the re-score **agrees with the recorded verdict on 11 of 12 rows and disagrees on one.** The
first OFF batch's repetition 2 was recorded as `2 failed, 1 passed`, and the same file passes all
three assertions today.

The cause is not drift, it is an instrument change. `59a575e` rewrote the structure guard to find
headings *by shape* — because unaided runs write every paragraph as `Normal` and build the hierarchy
with direct formatting, so a style-keyed guard read those documents as having no headings at all.
That landed at 13:51 on 2026-09-21. The first OFF batch ran at **13:07** and the second at 13:43.
So half the OFF baseline was scored by a guard that has since been fixed, and the row the fix was
written for is exactly the row that moves.

| scoring | OFF | on `as-installed` | on `v2` |
|---|---|---|---|
| as recorded, two verdicts | 0/6 | 0/3 | 2/3 |
| **current verdict, all 12 artifacts re-scored** | **1/6** | 0/3 | 2/3 |

**`docx-brand-arial-black`'s unaided baseline is therefore 1/6, not the 0/6 quoted in
[`docx-pptx-onarm-20260922.md`](docx-pptx-onarm-20260922.md).** The direction of every claim in §2
survives — 1/6 unaided against 3/3 on both scored rules is still the effect — but the baseline was
never zero, and a task whose OFF arm is 1/6 rather than 0/6 is a *marginal* cell, which changes how
its ON arm should be read (and matches `docxjs-table-dxa`, also 1/6).

**The apparatus gap this exposes:** a row records `skill_sha`, `py_venv`, `venv_packages` and
`node_packages`, but **nothing about the verdict that scored it**. The venv and the skill are pinned
precisely so that "a changed instrument" cannot be mistaken for "a changed result", and the verdict
is the most load-bearing instrument of the three. Recovering this took a git-log archaeology dig
against run timestamps; it should have been a field comparison. Fixed separately.
